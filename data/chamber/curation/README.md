# Chamber curation: plan

Phases 2 and 3 of `docs/chamber-music-plan.md`: research subagents turn the IMSLP census (`data/chamber/census/CENSUS.md`) into catalogue entries, one composer slice each, and add the twentieth-century and living composers IMSLP lacks. Three pilot slices have run (below); nothing here writes to `data/chamber/works.json` yet.

## Files
- `AGENT_BRIEF.md`: the brief every curation agent follows (scope, floor, one entry per work, benchmarks and cross-listing, the fields, house title style, nicknames and `aka`, alternatives and ad lib. parts, levels, sources, output format, working rules).
- `groups.json`: the phase-2 slices, generated by `npx tsx scripts/chamber/curation_slices.ts` from the census. A slice lists its composers (a big composer split by formation family: strings / piano / mixed), its IMSLP works and how many of them IMSLP links to a recording.
- `groups-modern.json`: the phase-3 slices, written by hand: 287 composers in 17 slices by region and school. These composers are left out of the phase-2 slices; their IMSLP candidates (most have none) are in their phase-3 input. `modern-coverage.json` shows each one's IMSLP count.
- `input/<slice>.json` (not in git): what one agent gets; write them with `npx tsx scripts/chamber/curation_slices.ts --inputs`.
- `out/<slice>.json`: the agents' results (the three pilot slices so far).

The census's floor sample found that 8 of 51 sampled works by tail composers are recorded after all (Scharwenka's Piano Quartet, Foerster's Nonet, Amanda Maier's quartet …), so the triage looks each composer up rather than dropping the tail on IMSLP's evidence.

Core is a composer with five or more works that IMSLP links to a commercial recording, a benchmark composer, or one of about 65 standard chamber composers that IMSLP's links under-count (`CORE_EXTRA` in the script: Jenkins, Lawes, Elgar, Sibelius, Arriaga, Weber, Krommer, Danzi …). Sizes: about 130 works for a core slice, 250 for a mid slice, 600 for a tail slice. With the three pilot slices done, that leaves **103 agents** at about 90 tool calls each, all Claude Code subagents (no API-key spend).

Three subagents ran the brief on one slice of each kind; their files are in `out/`, `npx tsx scripts/chamber/curation_check.ts` passes all three with no errors, and the results page is https://claude.ai/artifact/LPtNzoMoxeuTXPYyLJizP6 (private).

- Haydn: the 68 authentic quartets plus the quartet version of the *Seven Last Words*; nicknames and German `aka` throughout; levels 3 (Op. 1–2) to 6 (late quartets), consistent with the six benchmarks; no benchmark issues.
- The tail triage kept 8% of its works (the census sample predicted about 15% recorded) and proposed eight self-uploading living composers for `excluded-composers.json`.
- All three used well under the 90-call budget, so the slice sizes can stay.
- The brief has been updated with what the agents found: Presto and the Naxos Music Library refuse scripts, so recordings are checked in the Apple Music catalogue (iTunes Search API) and MusicBrainz; prefer single-work IMSLP pages; `"gap": true` for gap-check finds that are on IMSLP under another page; what counts in the tail triage; unfinished works performed as they stand; 18th-century divertimenti that IMSLP files under "strings".

Brief changes during the full run (made between waves, for problems several agents hit; each agent works from the brief as it stood when it started):
- After waves 1–2 (c01–c16; from c25 on):
  1. Census pages filed under "unspecified instruments" are checked too: baroque prints that name alternatives ("flutes, violins or oboes", "viols or violins") were filed there (Gibbons's *Fantazies of III Parts*, Dowland's *Lachrimae*, Boismortier Op. 37, Naudot Op. 17; c01, c10, c16).
  2. Baroque ensemble works without a soloist (concerti a quattro, sinfonie, balletti): in only when the source points to one player per part and a recording is one to a part; otherwise skipped as string orchestra and named in `note`. Wave 1–2 agents decided this case by case: Albinoni's *Balletti a cinque* (c07) and Telemann's TWV 43 concertos (c04) are in, Caldara's *Sinfonie a quattro* (c09), Dall'Abaco Op. 2 (c12), Valentini Op. 7 (c11), Tessarini Opp. 10–11 (c14) and Vivaldi RV 169 (c05) out. The owner may still rule.
  3. Accompanied keyboard sonatas whose first edition marks all the strings ad libitum: kept when recorded with the strings, with "Strings ad lib. in the first edition" at the start of `notes` (Avison, c08, kept them; Vanhal's Op. 12, c18, was skipped under the ad-lib rule).
  4. Renaissance and early-17th-century composers take the era `baroque` (the vocabulary has no earlier era; c01, c02).
  5. The budget counts tool calls: several agents stopped at 30–40 calls with composers left partial (c01 needed a follow-up).
- After wave 4 (from c34 on): the Sources section tells agents that Apple and MusicBrainz answer 403/429 when about eight agents query at once: wait and retry, space requests, and never count a blocked lookup as "none found" (c31, c41).
- Not changed, for the owner to rule on: a separate bass-viol or cello part beside the continuo is counted as a part by some agents (Purcell, Legrenzi, Gentili, Pez, Rebel, Williams) and folded into the continuo by others (Richter, Franceschini); phase 5 should settle it with the sources.
- Not changed, for the merge to settle: keyboard works of the 1750s–60s whose print names only the harpsichord (Avison, J. C. Bach Opp. 2 and 4, Abel Op. 2, Telemann-era sonatas) are entered as `harpsichord`, formation "other", with piano as an alternative where the print allows it; later accompanied keyboard works (Haydn, Boccherini, Clementi, J. C. Bach Op. 15) are `piano` with the harpsichord as the alternative, formation piano trio or quartet. One rule for both would make the formation filters consistent.

## Proposed order
1. The owner's decisions of 2026-10-16 are in the brief and the census (`CENSUS.md`, "Decisions"): vocal works only as chamber music, viol consorts in (their own formation), contemporary and living composers in when they have a professional track record (Stephen W. Beatty left out: `data/chamber/excluded-composers.json`), the glass harmonica as a note, family-name-first names accepted.
2. **Pilot of three slices**: `c22_haydn-string-quartet-large-strings` (six benchmarks to match against), one mid slice (`m07_early-romantic-mayseder-veit`) and one tail slice (`t07_classical-c-f`). Check the output against the brief, the budget, the floor decisions and the house style; resize the slices if the agents run out of calls (tail slices are the risk).
3. Core slices, then mid, then tail, about eight at a time; then the 17 modern slices, which can run alongside since no composer is in two slices.
4. A merge script (not written yet) collects `out/*.json`, drops duplicates against `works.json` and between slices, and hands the new works to phase 4 (grading) and 5 (verification) as `docs/chamber-music-plan.md` describes.
