# Chamber music section: plan

A second section of Arco Repertoire, reached from the instrument switcher at the top, that is as comprehensive and as carefully graded as the violin catalogue. Written 2026-10-15; the five decisions below were settled on 2026-10-16.

## Starting point
- The switcher (`components/InstrumentSwitcher.tsx`) only shows the other instruments as "Coming soon".
- Everything assumes one violin catalogue: `Piece` in `lib/types.ts` has violin-only facets (`type`, `settings`), the finder sentence in `lib/vocab.ts` reads "with piano / for solo violin", `lib/search.ts` treats "violin" as filler, and the advisor prompt says "violin works".
- The research machinery is reusable: the three October 2026 expansions (research subagents, two blind graders, independent verification, YouTube judging) ran on Claude Code subagents with no API-key spend.
- Overlap with the violin catalogue is small. The modern expansion excluded "chamber works for three or more different instruments"; the overlap is the 35 baroque trio sonatas (Handel, Vivaldi, Locatelli) and Chausson's *Concert* Op. 21.
- Size: the local IMSLP cache (`data/cache/imslp-all/pages/`, only the ~1,000 composers harvested for violin, large catalogues partly) already holds 2,538 original works for 3–9 players, 2,276 with a string part (916 string quartets, 276 piano trios). A rough guess is 4,000–5,000 works after curation, about the size of the violin catalogue (4,883); the phase-2 census replaces the guess with a number.

## Decisions (settled 2026-10-16)
1. **Scope.** Works for 3–9 players, one player per part, with at least one string instrument: string trios to octets, piano trios, quartets and quintets, strings with winds (clarinet quintets, the Schubert Octet, the Beethoven Septet), baroque trio and quartet sonatas with continuo, and strings with harp, guitar or voice. Ensembles without strings (wind quintets, brass) wait for those instruments' own sections. Duos stay in the instrument sections. Works for violins alone (2–4 violins) stay in the violin section; with continuo they are listed in both. **Floor** (settled 2026-10-16): only works that are performed or recorded as concert repertoire. Easy pedagogical ensemble music that is not usually performed (teaching collections, method-book ensembles, school arrangements) is out, even when it is original; an easy work that is genuinely performed (Pärt's *Summa*, Corelli's trio sonatas, Pachelbel's Canon) is in. Level 1 may stay nearly empty, and that is expected.
2. **What a level means.** One level on the violin catalogue's 1–10 scale: the level the hardest part asks of its player, raised by up to one level when the ensemble demands (coordination, exposed intonation, stamina, balance) go beyond the notes. For a part on another instrument the scale reads as the same exam grade on that instrument (level 4 ≈ ABRSM grade 7 piano, cello or clarinet). Each work also names its **hardest part** ("violin 1", "piano", "clarinet") or says "balanced". Levels per part may come later for the ~500 most-played works.
3. **Granularity.** One entry per work, as in the violin catalogue: each quartet or trio is its own entry, with the opus as its set (Op. 76 No. 3 "Emperor" in the set of Op. 76). Movements are not split. Haydn's baryton trios are out; huge minor outputs (Boccherini's 100+ quintets) are in only when published or recorded, and are marked lesser-known.
4. **Addresses.** The section's home is `/chamber`. Piece pages keep `/piece/[id]/[slug]`; chamber ids start at 100001 so they never collide with violin ids. Composer pages show both sections. Cross-listed works (trio sonatas, Chausson) are one entry shown in both.
5. **Source of truth.** JSON in the repository (`data/chamber/`); no Google Sheet.

## Schema (phase 0)
- `lib/chamber/types.ts` and `lib/chamber/vocab.ts`: the work record, the formations, forms and instruments, and `formationFor()`, which derives the formation from the instrumentation.
- `data/chamber/works.json`: the catalogue. Each work has `id`, `composer` ("Surname, Given names", spelled as in the violin catalogue), `title` (house style, nickname in `("…")` at the end), `aka` (other names people search for), `formation`, `instruments` (instrument → count), `alternatives` (published substitutes for a part: the Kegelstatt Trio's clarinet may be a violin, the Brahms Horn Trio's horn a viola or cello) and `optional` (ad lib. parts such as the bass in *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*; neither counts toward players or formation, but both count for an "includes instrument" filter), `players` (parts; continuo counts as one, electronics as none), `form` (`work` = complete work or a single movement over about 12 minutes · `short` = a single shorter piece · `set` = a collection of pieces), `level`, `hardestPart`, `year`, `minutes`, `set`, `notes`, `popularity`, and the tag fields `mode`, `pace`, `character` the graders fill in later. `violinId` links a cross-listed violin entry (title and level must match it). Benchmark works carry `benchmark` ("proposed" or "approved") and `basis` (why that level).
- `data/chamber/composers.json`: dates, nationality, gender and era for composers who are not in the violin catalogue. Composers who are there take their metadata from the violin rows, so the two sections never disagree.
- `npx tsx scripts/chamber/check.ts` validates both files: scope (3–9 players, a string part), players matching the instruments, formation matching `formationFor()` unless a `formationNote` explains why (Schoenberg's Second Quartet has a soprano), the hardest part being one of the instruments, cross-listed entries agreeing with the violin catalogue.

## Phases
0. **Schema and benchmark works.** About 130 standard works with proposed levels, spread across formations, eras and levels, which every grader calibrates against. The owner sets or approves their levels. *Status 2026-10-16:* schema, validator and 135 benchmarks in `data/chamber/works.json`. The proposed levels were graded again blind by Opus and Sonnet subagents (`data/chamber/benchmarks/GRADER_BRIEF.md`, `grades/`). Where all three were within one level (121 of 132), the level is the median and the work is `approved`; the three works also in the violin catalogue keep their violin levels. For the 11 works two levels apart the owner chose the level on a private review page (https://claude.ai/artifact/RiJxzCWbnY6KNp8998E9Gk; record in `benchmarks/owner-picks.json`), so all 135 benchmarks are now `approved`. Hardest part: the graders' answer where both agreed against the proposal. Mode, pace and character come from the two graders, combined as in `scripts/expand/modern_merge.ts`. Every entry was fact-checked by three agents against IMSLP, publishers and Wikipedia (`VERIFY_BRIEF.md`, `verify/`): 108 confirmed, 22 corrected, 5 doubts left as they were (Hoffstetter's year, the Black Angels duration, the D. 898 year, Schoenfield's year, Boccherini's castanets; the unproven "castanets ad lib." was removed).
1. **Site changes** (in parallel with phases 2–5): `Piece` belongs to one or more sections and gains the chamber fields; a separate `public/data/chamber.json` (the violin file is already 3.6 MB); chamber vocabulary for the finder sentence ("Show me short pieces for string quartet around level 3"); an "includes instrument" filter; search by nickname and German or original titles ("Death and the Maiden", "Der Tod und das Mädchen", "Trout", "Archduke", "Dumky"); rows that show the instrumentation; a working switcher with "Chamber music" under an "Ensembles" divider. Hidden until launch; mockups first in `design/`.
2. **Census and canon.** Extend `scripts/expand/imslp_harvest_all.py` from composers to scoring categories ("For 2 violins, viola, cello", "For violin, cello, piano", "For 3 players" …) so the public-domain repertoire arrives with opus, key, scoring, year and IMSLP link. Subagents curate by composer slice (drop arrangements, fragments and unpublished works; set nicknames, sets, formation). A completeness check against standard work lists for about 150 core chamber composers. *Status 2026-10-16:* census done (`scripts/chamber/imslp_harvest.py`, `scripts/chamber/imslp_census.ts`; results and open questions in `data/chamber/census/CENSUS.md`): 23,230 in-scope works on IMSLP, of which about 7,600 instrumental works pass the floor (a 120-page sample checked against recording catalogues; 95% range 5,900–9,300), so the section will be nearer 9,000 works than the 4,000–5,000 guessed above. The curation brief and the slices are drafted for review (`data/chamber/curation/`); no curation agent has run.
3. **Modern and contemporary.** Publisher-catalogue research slices, as in `data/expansion/modern/AGENT_BRIEF.md`. The slowest phase.
4. **Grading.** Researcher estimate plus two blind graders (Opus and Sonnet) calibrated on the benchmarks, median of the three; the same graders give mode, pace and character. A pilot of about 200 works first, to check the spread.
5. **Verification.** A second, independent agent re-checks every addition; full checks for low-confidence entries and unusual scorings; duplicates merged; ids assigned.
6. **Enrichment.** Recordings through `scripts/collect_recordings.py` and the reviewer judging of `scripts/judge-recordings.ts` (queries need the composer and catalogue number: "String Quartet No. 3" alone is ambiguous); IMSLP links from phase 2; durations.
7. **Advisor and launch.** A chamber advisor prompt and catalogue text that ask about players and instruments; sitemap, preview image, intro line; the switcher entry goes live; a write-up in `docs/`.

## Cost and risks
- All research, grading and judging through Claude Code subagents, as before; no API-key spend. The one new running cost is a second cached advisor prompt (Haiku).
- **Level meaning** is the main risk. If the pilot's graders spread widely, the benchmarks need work before the rest is graded.
- **Prolific minor composers** (Boccherini, Pleyel, Vanhal, Haydn) could swamp the lists; the popularity flag and the default sort have to keep that in check.
- **Instrument names on IMSLP** vary ("violone", "basso", "keyboard"): one converter maps them, and the build fails on a name it does not know, as `data/accompaniment-map.json` does for accompaniments.
- **A thin low end.** With the floor above, few works sit at levels 1–3, so groups of young players will find less here than young soloists do in the violin section.
