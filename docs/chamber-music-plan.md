# Chamber music section: plan

A second section of Arco Repertoire, reached from the instrument switcher at the top, that is as comprehensive and as carefully graded as the violin catalogue. Written 2026-10-15; the five decisions below were settled on 2026-10-16.

**Working rule** (owner, 2026-10-16): all chamber work sits on the local `chamber` branch (the mockups, the hidden `/chamber` site, the census and the curation). No pull requests, and nothing goes to main until the owner calls the section an MVP.

## Starting point
- The switcher (`components/InstrumentSwitcher.tsx`) only shows the other instruments as "Coming soon".
- Everything assumes one violin catalogue: `Piece` in `lib/types.ts` has violin-only facets (`type`, `settings`), the finder sentence in `lib/vocab.ts` reads "with piano / for solo violin", `lib/search.ts` treats "violin" as filler, and the advisor prompt says "violin works".
- The research machinery is reusable: the three October 2026 expansions (research subagents, two blind graders, independent verification, YouTube judging) ran on Claude Code subagents with no API-key spend.
- Overlap with the violin catalogue is small. The modern expansion excluded "chamber works for three or more different instruments"; the overlap is the 35 baroque trio sonatas (Handel, Vivaldi, Locatelli) and Chausson's *Concert* Op. 21.
- Size: the local IMSLP cache (`data/cache/imslp-all/pages/`, only the ~1,000 composers harvested for violin, large catalogues partly) already holds 2,538 original works for 3–9 players, 2,276 with a string part (916 string quartets, 276 piano trios). A rough guess is 4,000–5,000 works after curation, about the size of the violin catalogue (4,883); the phase-2 census replaces the guess with a number.

## Decisions (settled 2026-10-16)
1. **Scope.** Works for 3–9 players, one player per part, with at least one string instrument: string trios to octets, piano trios, quartets and quintets, strings with winds (clarinet quintets, the Schubert Octet, the Beethoven Septet), baroque trio and quartet sonatas with continuo, and strings with harp, guitar or voice. Ensembles without strings (wind quintets, brass) wait for those instruments' own sections. Duos stay in the instrument sections. Works for violins alone (2–4 violins) stay in the violin section; with continuo they are listed in both. **Floor** (settled 2026-10-16): only works that are performed or recorded as concert repertoire. Easy pedagogical ensemble music that is not usually performed (teaching collections, method-book ensembles, school arrangements) is out, even when it is original; an easy work that is genuinely performed (Pärt's *Summa*, Corelli's trio sonatas, Pachelbel's Canon) is in. Level 1 may stay nearly empty, and that is expected. **Settled after the census** (2026-10-16): vocal works only as chamber music (a voice with a chamber ensemble of three or more instruments; songs with one obbligato instrument, cantatas with continuo and folk-song settings are out); viol consorts are in, as their own formation ("Viol consort": three or more viols, with or without organ or harpsichord); contemporary and living composers are in when they have a track record of works performed and taken seriously by professional musicians, and self-published composers are out (`data/chamber/excluded-composers.json`, starting with Stephen W. Beatty); an instrument the vocabulary lacks becomes a note on the piece, not a new instrument (the glass harmonica of Mozart's K. 617 is listed as celesta, its usual substitute today); solo showpieces published with string-quartet accompaniment (Mayseder, Lipiński) are in, under "Other ensembles" with a `formationNote`.
2. **What a level means.** One level on the violin catalogue's 1–10 scale: the level the hardest part asks of its player, raised by up to one level when the ensemble demands (coordination, exposed intonation, stamina, balance) go beyond the notes. For a part on another instrument the scale reads as the same exam grade on that instrument (level 4 ≈ ABRSM grade 7 piano, cello or clarinet). Each work also names its **hardest part** ("violin 1", "piano", "clarinet") or says "balanced". Levels per part may come later for the ~500 most-played works.
3. **Granularity.** One entry per work, as in the violin catalogue: each quartet or trio is its own entry, with the opus as its set (Op. 76 No. 3 "Emperor" in the set of Op. 76). Movements are not split. Haydn's baryton trios are out; huge minor outputs (Boccherini's 100+ quintets) are in only when published or recorded, and are marked lesser-known.
4. **Addresses.** The section's home is `/chamber`. Piece pages keep `/piece/[id]/[slug]`; chamber ids start at 100001 so they never collide with violin ids. Composer pages show both sections. Cross-listed works (trio sonatas, Chausson) are one entry shown in both.
5. **Source of truth.** JSON in the repository (`data/chamber/`); no Google Sheet.

## Schema (phase 0)
- `lib/chamber/types.ts` and `lib/chamber/vocab.ts`: the work record, the formations, forms and instruments, and `formationFor()`, which derives the formation from the instrumentation.
- `data/chamber/works.json`: the catalogue. Each work has `id`, `composer` ("Surname, Given names", or family name first for the Chinese names the violin catalogue writes that way, such as "Tan Dun"; spelled as in the violin catalogue), `title` (house style, nickname in `("…")` at the end), `aka` (other names people search for), `formation`, `instruments` (instrument → count), `alternatives` (published substitutes for a part: the Kegelstatt Trio's clarinet may be a violin, the Brahms Horn Trio's horn a viola or cello) and `optional` (ad lib. parts such as the bass in *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*; neither counts toward players or formation, but both count for an "includes instrument" filter), `players` (parts; continuo counts as one, electronics as none), `form` (`work` = complete work or a single movement over about 12 minutes · `short` = a single shorter piece · `set` = a collection of pieces), `level`, `hardestPart`, `year`, `minutes`, `set`, `notes`, `popularity`, and the tag fields `mode`, `pace`, `character` the graders fill in later. `violinId` links a cross-listed violin entry (title and level must match it). Benchmark works carry `benchmark` ("proposed" or "approved") and `basis` (why that level).
- `data/chamber/composers.json`: dates, nationality, gender and era for composers who are not in the violin catalogue. Composers who are there take their metadata from the violin rows, so the two sections never disagree.
- `npx tsx scripts/chamber/check.ts` validates both files: scope (3–9 players, a string part), players matching the instruments, formation matching `formationFor()` unless a `formationNote` explains why (Schoenberg's Second Quartet has a soprano), the hardest part being one of the instruments, cross-listed entries agreeing with the violin catalogue.

## Phases
0. **Schema and benchmark works.** About 130 standard works with proposed levels, spread across formations, eras and levels, which every grader calibrates against. The owner sets or approves their levels. *Status 2026-10-16:* schema, validator and 135 benchmarks in `data/chamber/works.json`. The proposed levels were graded again blind by Opus and Sonnet subagents (`data/chamber/benchmarks/GRADER_BRIEF.md`, `grades/`). Where all three were within one level (121 of 132), the level is the median and the work is `approved`; the three works also in the violin catalogue keep their violin levels. For the 11 works two levels apart the owner chose the level on a private review page (https://claude.ai/artifact/RiJxzCWbnY6KNp8998E9Gk; record in `benchmarks/owner-picks.json`), so all 135 benchmarks are now `approved`. Hardest part: the graders' answer where both agreed against the proposal. Mode, pace and character come from the two graders, combined as in `scripts/expand/modern_merge.ts`. Every entry was fact-checked by three agents against IMSLP, publishers and Wikipedia (`VERIFY_BRIEF.md`, `verify/`): 108 confirmed, 22 corrected, 5 doubts left as they were (Hoffstetter's year, the Black Angels duration, the D. 898 year, Schoenfield's year, Boccherini's castanets; the unproven "castanets ad lib." was removed).
1. **Site changes** (in parallel with phases 2–5): `Piece` belongs to one or more sections and gains the chamber fields; a separate `public/data/chamber.json` (the violin file is already 3.6 MB); chamber vocabulary for the finder sentence ("Show me short pieces for string quartet around level 3"); an "includes instrument" filter; search by nickname and German or original titles ("Death and the Maiden", "Der Tod und das Mädchen", "Trout", "Archduke", "Dumky"); rows that show the instrumentation; a working switcher with "Chamber music" under an "Ensembles" divider. Hidden until launch; mockups first in `design/`. *Status 2026-10-16:* built and hidden. The owner chose from `design/chamber-options.html` the row where the level pebble names the part that sets it (B) and the instrument chips under the level bars (2); the switcher and the sentence as drawn. `PieceBase` in `lib/types.ts` carries `sections` and `chamber`; `scripts/build-chamber.ts` writes `public/data/chamber.json` (works also in the violin catalogue keep their violin id, address and recording); `/chamber` has the finder, the "includes" chips (alternatives and ad lib. parts count, several mean all), the shared toolbar and results; chamber-only works have piece pages at `/piece/[id]/[slug]`; search reads `aka` as strongly as titles, and "violin" counts as a word there. Until `lib/chamber/launch.ts` says otherwise, `/chamber` and its piece pages are noindex and out of the sitemap, the switcher shows the Ensembles entry only on chamber pages, and composer pages list violin works only; at launch they list chamber works too and chamber-only composers get pages. The violin pages render as before (every prerendered page compared with a build of `main`).
2. **Census and canon.** Extend `scripts/expand/imslp_harvest_all.py` from composers to scoring categories ("For 2 violins, viola, cello", "For violin, cello, piano", "For 3 players" …) so the public-domain repertoire arrives with opus, key, scoring, year and IMSLP link. Subagents curate by composer slice (drop arrangements, fragments and unpublished works; set nicknames, sets, formation). A completeness check against standard work lists for about 150 core chamber composers. *Status 2026-10-16:* census done (`scripts/chamber/imslp_harvest.py`, `scripts/chamber/imslp_census.ts`; results and the owner's decisions in `data/chamber/census/CENSUS.md`): 21,074 in-scope works on IMSLP, of which about 7,600 instrumental works pass the floor (a 120-page sample checked against recording catalogues; 95% range 6,000–9,300), plus about 300 viol consorts and chamber vocal works, so the section will be nearer 9,000 works than the 4,000–5,000 guessed above. The curation brief and the 88 phase-2 and 17 phase-3 slices are in `data/chamber/curation/`. A pilot of three slices (Haydn's quartets, an early-Romantic mid slice, a Classical tail triage) kept 69, 62 and 47 works, passed `scripts/chamber/curation_check.ts`, and its lessons are in the brief (`data/chamber/curation/README.md`).
3. **Modern and contemporary.** Publisher-catalogue research slices, as in `data/expansion/modern/AGENT_BRIEF.md`. The slowest phase. *Status 2026-10-24:* the phase-2 and phase-3 slices are curated and merged by `scripts/chamber/merge_curation.ts` (8,302 works beside the 135 benchmarks, 43 of them also in the violin catalogue; ids in `id-registry.json`, each work's evidence in `sources.json`).
4. **Grading.** Researcher estimate plus two blind graders (Opus and Sonnet) calibrated on the benchmarks, median of the three; the same graders give mode, pace and character. A pilot of about 200 works first, to check the spread. *Status 2026-10-24:* done (`scripts/chamber/grading_prep.ts`, `grading_apply.ts`, `data/chamber/grading/`). Grader A matched the researcher on 60% of works; grader B ran only where they differed, and the level is the median of the three (spread 0 on 4,946 works, 1 on 3,025, 2 on 277, 3 on 11). 2,041 levels moved and 343 hardest parts changed.
5. **Verification.** A second, independent agent re-checks every addition; full checks for low-confidence entries and unusual scorings; duplicates merged; ids assigned. *Status 2026-10-24:* done as a tiered check (`scripts/chamber/imslp_crosscheck.ts`, `verify_prep.ts`, `verify_apply.ts`, `data/chamber/verify/BRIEF.md`): every work whose IMSLP page disagrees with it, every medium- or low-confidence, single-source or IMSLP-less entry, and a random 300 of the rest, 4,737 works in all. 3,550 confirmed, 993 corrected (mostly years, notes and durations; 90 titles, 53 scorings), 13 removed (`verify/rejected.json`), 181 doubts left unchanged for the owner (`verify/doubts.json`). About 12% of the random sample needed a correction, almost all minor; the remaining ~3,400 high-confidence works were not checked.
6. **Enrichment.** Recordings through `scripts/collect_recordings.py` and the reviewer judging of `scripts/judge-recordings.ts` (queries need the composer and catalogue number: "String Quartet No. 3" alone is ambiguous); IMSLP links from phase 2; durations. *Status 2026-10-24:* recordings done (`scripts/chamber/collect_recordings.py`, `scripts/chamber/recordings.ts`, `data/chamber/recordings/BRIEF.md`): YouTube candidates for every chamber-only work, Sonnet judges preferring a clean upload and accepting an album or concert that includes the work, and an oEmbed check that the pick embeds. 5,389 works have a high- or medium-confidence video (227 of them within albums or concerts); 215 low-confidence picks are left out and 2,769 works have no acceptable video.
7. **Advisor and launch.** A chamber advisor prompt and catalogue text that ask about players and instruments; sitemap, preview image, intro line; the switcher entry goes live; a write-up in `docs/`. *Status 2026-10-25:* the advisor is built. `/api/advisor` takes `section: "chamber"` and answers with its own prompt (`lib/advisor/chamber-prompt.ts`), its own cached catalogue (`lib/advisor/chamber-catalogue-text.ts`, written by `scripts/build-chamber.ts`: the 1,175 best-known works under an 85K-character budget, every ensemble's best-known 30 first, ranked by the well-known mark, approved benchmarks and recording views; the prompt and tools come to about 62K tokens, so a conversation has several turns before Haiku's 100K-token price step) and its own tools (`lib/advisor/chamber-tools.ts`). `match_ensemble` works out what a group plays, one player to a part and nobody left out (`lib/chamber/ensemble.ts`: published alternatives, ad lib. parts, a cello doubling the continuo; a piano on the continuo or a harpsichord part and a continuo bass without keyboard count as substitutes and are listed after the plain fits), and with `add` or `sit_out` what one or two more players, or one fewer, would open up. `/chamber` has the Ask dock and panel, the advisor's picks above the results, and "Ask about this piece" in the side sheet; the header's Ask button opens the chamber advisor on chamber pages. Tried on 21 questions against the API (about $0.18): group fits, adding or losing a player, moods, occasions, a duo (redirected kindly), "what next" and follow-ups; every id it linked was checked against the catalogue (one text link named K. 575 but pointed to K. 589, which the prompt now guards against). *Launch prep, 2026-10-25:* `lib/chamber/launch.ts` now has the section live unless `NEXT_PUBLIC_CHAMBER_LIVE=0`: "Chamber music" under Ensembles in the switcher everywhere, `/chamber`, the chamber piece pages and 846 chamber-only composer pages in the sitemap (15,035 addresses) and indexed, composer pages listing chamber works ("1 violin work and 253 chamber works by Luigi Boccherini"), and `/composers` listing 1,797 composers. `/chamber` and the chamber piece pages share their own preview card (`app/chamber/opengraph-image.tsx`, drawn by `components/OgCard.tsx` like the violin card, which is unchanged byte for byte). The violin advisor now points questions about groups to the chamber section. Not merged into `main` or pushed yet.

## Cost and risks
- All research, grading and judging through Claude Code subagents, as before; no API-key spend. The one new running cost is a second cached advisor prompt (Haiku).
- **Level meaning** is the main risk. If the pilot's graders spread widely, the benchmarks need work before the rest is graded.
- **Prolific minor composers** (Boccherini, Pleyel, Vanhal, Haydn) could swamp the lists; the popularity flag and the default sort have to keep that in check.
- **Instrument names on IMSLP** vary ("violone", "basso", "keyboard"): one converter maps them, and the build fails on a name it does not know, as `data/accompaniment-map.json` does for accompaniments.
- **A thin low end.** With the floor above, few works sit at levels 1–3, so groups of young players will find less here than young soloists do in the violin section.
