Composer groups, refined

No blobs. Each version shows one way to display the level that looks the same for a single level (“4”) and a range (“5–8”), and one way to mark a set you can open. Partita No. 2 is shown open; Sonata No. 1 is closed. Tags are sample values.

A · Numerals + an open button Recommended

The level is just a number in its level colour, so a range is simply two numbers (each in its own colour). The button column does the action: ▶ plays a single work, ⌄ opens a set. The set's title says how many entries it holds; the open set's entries hang off a thin line.

B · Scale track + “6 entries” toggle

The level is a tiny 10-step track, the same as the finder's bars. A single level lights one step and a range lights a span, so both read as a position on the same scale. Sets get an underlined “6 entries” toggle under the title, in the same style as the site's other links.

C · Level blocks + a stack-of-sheets tab

The level becomes a small solid block, echoing the coloured “LEVEL 5” picker in the finder. A range is the same block, just wider, with its two colours blended. Sets get a tiny stack-of-sheets mark with the count in front of the title: rectangular and line-drawn, so it can't be mistaken for a level.

My pick is A. It's the cleanest. A plain number in its level colour handles single levels and ranges identically, nothing on the left competes with the title, and the right-hand button tells you what the row does: play it, or open the set. If you'd like the level to feel more visual, B's track is the one I'd choose next.
