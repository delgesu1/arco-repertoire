Composer groups, round two

The pebbles are back for levels, and sets are never a pebble: the ⌄ button on the right is what opens them. Each version tries a different way to show a set's level range as a pebble, and a different way to lay out the opened entries, all without guide lines or fades. Partita No. 2 is open, Sonata No. 1 is closed.

1 · Pebble pair + a tray Recommended

A range is two overlapping pebbles, the lowest and highest level, so it uses exactly the same shape as a single level. Opening a set pulls out a tray: the set and its entries sit together on a lighter sheet, numbered, each with its own pebble and play button.

2 · Stretched pebble + a concert programme

A range is one pebble stretched sideways, its colour sliding from the lowest level to the highest. The opened entries read like a printed concert programme: movement numbers, dotted leaders to the length, then a small pebble and play button. No boxes, no lines between entries.

3 · Pebble scatter + movement tiles

A set shows a little scatter of tiny pebbles, one for each level inside it, with the range written underneath, so you see the spread at a glance. Opening a set lays its entries out as small tiles, three across, with the complete work spanning the top.

4 · “From” pebble + soft striped list

A set shows a single pebble with its starting level and a small “+” (“5+”, meaning from level 5 up), so every row has exactly one pebble. Opened entries stay in the table's columns, indented, with soft alternating bands instead of lines.

My pick is 1. The pebble pair keeps one shape language (a range is just two pebbles), and the tray answers the “strange lines and fade” problem: an opened set reads as one object you pulled out, and it folds back cleanly. If you want something airier, 2's concert-programme listing is the most characterful alternative.
